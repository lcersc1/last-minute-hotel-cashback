## Why last-minute hotels are the best case for cash back

Hotels pay affiliates well because the commission structure in accommodation has always been generous. Flights pay almost nothing — a $900 plane ticket typically returns about $9 through a cashback portal — while hotels, vacation rentals and bundled trips pay properly. That asymmetry matters for last-minute travelers: the component you're scrambling to book (the room) is precisely the one that pays.

The math on a typical late booking looks like this:

- A **$200/night IHG property** at 12% cash back returns **$24 per night**.
- A **$1,000 Trip.com hotel booking** at 8% returns **$80**.
- A **$500 Hotels.com order** returns roughly **$20** at its recent 4% rate.
- Over a year, $1,500 of hotel spending at 7% is **$105**; at 12% it's **$180**.

None of this replaces hunting for a good last-minute rate. It stacks on top of it. You find the cheapest available room the same way you always would — then route the booking through a portal and collect a percentage of the room rate back in real money.

## The rules that void your cash back (last-minute bookers hit these most)

Rushed bookings are where tracking dies. These are the specific failure modes, several of which only appear in the fine print:

- **Using Safari.** TopCashback's own IHG page states that cash back tracking cannot be guaranteed on Safari. Book through Chrome or another browser.
- **Using the booking site's app.** The tracking session lives in your browser. If you click through from the portal and then finish in the hotel chain's app, the trail is broken.
- **Applying outside coupon codes.** Non-affiliated codes can void the merchant's commission entirely, and the cash back with it. Some merchants allow code stacking, some don't — check the terms on the merchant page before checkout.
- **Wrong rate types.** Marriott Bonvoy member-exclusive rates are generally excluded from cash back, and points-redemption stays earn nothing (there's no cash spent). Standard and promotional rates usually qualify, though promotional rates may carry a slightly different percentage.
- **Pay-at-hotel rates.** If you book a "pay at the hotel" rate, cash back is calculated only on the amount paid online at booking, not the full stay value. On last-minute Trip.com bookings, prepaid rates maximize the rebate.
- **Modifying or rebooking.** Change your reservation after booking and the original tracking often dies. Cancel, and the cash back is gone. This stings most on refundable last-minute rooms — book once, correctly.
- **Ad blockers and disabled cookies.** The single most common cause of lost tracking. Turn the blocker off before you click through.

If cash back does fail to track despite clean technique, a support ticket with your order number and date usually gets it manually credited. It works often enough to be worthwhile; you just might need to nudge support occasionally.

## Do you still earn hotel points? Mostly yes — with one exception

Booking through a portal earns you the hotel's own loyalty points on direct-chain bookings (IHG One Rewards, Hilton Honors, Marriott Bonvoy), because from the hotel's perspective you booked on their site. The cash back and the points stack.

The exception is OTA bookings. Reserving through Expedia, Hotels.com, Priceline or similar third-party sites usually means **no hotel points and no elite night credit**. On a $300 last-minute room, giving up maybe 300 points and zero elite nights for $15–36 in cash is a good trade. On a $2,000 stay where you're chasing status, it may not be. Trip.com is the interesting middle case: its Trip Coins rewards and TopCashback cash back are two separate systems that stack on the same order.

**Does cash back work on same-day hotel bookings?**
Yes. The booking date doesn't matter; what matters is that the reservation completes in the tracked browser session. A same-day booking actually confirms to "payable" faster than one made months ahead, since there's no long wait for the stay to complete.
